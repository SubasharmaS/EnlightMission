Key facts

The origin of the word “Irula” is not clear. The word “irul” means darkness / night in Tamil and is possibly link to their skin

The Irulas are widely spread in different states of the south of India (3mln in total); in Tamil Nadu state is around 150.000 people, living now in around 100 villages

Women stay in the village to take care of the children and the housework, while men leave the village for six months to work. They are making bricks or work as coolies in the rice paddys - by working as ploughmen in the fields of the landlords during the sowing and harvesting seasons or by working in the rice mills. They also do some work in the landlord’s house. Some of them also collect fire wood from the forest to sell.

History

As a tribe, used to live in the forests and they are traditional snake and rat catchers. This is no longer their means of living and over these years of existence they have been unable to find a sustainable occupation for themselves

When they were living in the forests, they used to sell products like honey, with which they made wax, firewood and other forest’s products. In return, they got village products for their use. They obtained their food mainly from the forest and the wild animals living in it

Following the forest protection bill of 1976, the traditional homes and lifestyles of the Irulas has become illegal. They started to move to neighbors villages with the hope of rebuilding their lives. They were segregate by other populations, because if we consider the caste system, the are even lower than the “untouchable”

The Irula’s — main problems of the tribe

Life facilities
houses made out of mud; destroyed by the mousson after 3 months of build; limitted access to water, electricity and education
Jobs
not skilled to do any kind of job; some of them are not paid with money, but only with products
Education
girls are not allowed tp go to schools; boys do, but they can’t expect the support or of their parents. They are not given the schedule tribe certificate that may help them to study. The consequence is that even if they manage to go through the exams, they don’t get any certificates
Social neglect
the are even lower than the lowest cast, even than the “untouchable”. They are cheated, abused and neglected by people and the government. Irula’s are cut off from the society and don’t even know the benefits that they are eligible to receive as a tribe in India. For the owners, they would be losing extremely cheap ploughmen to use
Lands certificates
they don’t even have a land certificate for the place the stay in
Tribes certificates
being extremelly shy and scared of outside world, they are unable to draw any benefits that may be forthcoming to them from the government
Psychological conditions
the fear of the outside world makes them to continue their livelihood

Today, we already achieve to give Irula people 3000 cast certificates and 700 lands grants are being processed.
